# Development timeline — archived evidence, not reconstructed footage

**October 2026 — Three.js forest park on an RTX 5060 Laptop GPU.** The exact starting date and initial launch screenshot are unconfirmed.

| Date | Evidence-backed summary | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 3 | ~11 m forest test. Procedural tree forms, grass, ferns, rocks and PBR materials; tuning sun, exposure and contrast. | Images are before/after lighting changes, not the first launch. |
| Oct 4 | Preserved the reference area and extended it to a ~100 × 150 m forest, then a finite 1 × 1 km layout using 50 m cells (20 × 20). | Fifteen walk-through checkpoints do not establish uniform quality or 60 fps throughout. |
| Oct 4–7 | Experimented with batching, LOD, reduced GPU uploads and distant-tree proxies, including measured regressions. | Local experiments were not necessarily adopted throughout. |
| Oct 7 | Saved 1080p/high-quality measurements of the larger world. | No verified all-area 60 fps on the laptop. |
| Oct 16 | Refined forest floor and trails, preserving irregular grass and small stones. | Based on archived reports, not a full chat export. |
| Oct 17 | Prepared handoff documents for desktop continuation. | Exact PC changeover date is not verified. |

## Production method

1. Make a small area visually convincing.
2. Keep the accepted vegetation placement stable when extending it.
3. Recheck visual quality and performance after a change.
4. Reject experiments that regress; apply successful changes more broadly.

The later Unreal Engine 5 forest was separately recreated from scratch. According to the creator, this Three.js result was supplied as a visual reference and the UE5 work began in a similar roughly 100 m test area. That is **creator testimony**, not an independently audited UE5 development record.
